# Do Greater — Workshop 01 Orientation
## Getting Started with AI for Small Business
**Track:** small-business-ai-acmi
**Location:** Sparkhouse Charlotte  \
**Ops contact:** Amanda
**Duration:** 80 minutes (4 × 20-minute blocks) + 10-minute buffer  \
**Audience:** Small business owners with basic digital literacy, no AI deployment experience  \
**Framing:** MAD + EASY + ACMI + AI Integration  \
**ACMI alignment:** v1.5, madez namespace, string profile/signals, zset timeline

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. **Define** what AI can realistically do for a small business right now.
2. **Identify** one "quick-win" AI task they could implement this week.
3. **Explain** how ACMI-style persistent memory prevents AI from forgetting context.
4. **Draft** a 30-day AI integration roadmap for their own business.
5. **Classify** their daily workflows into MAD, EASY, ACMI, and AI Integration quadrants.

## Lesson Block 1 (20 min): Welcome & The Four Quadrants

**Theme:** MAP THE LANDSCAPE  
**Goal:** Orient participants to the MAD + EASY + ACMI + AI Integration framework and get them to label one real task from their own business.

### Facilitator Talking Points

- "If you've ever asked ChatGPT to write an email and got something weirdly generic, that's not a failure of AI — it's a failure of context. Today we fix that."
- "MAD means we break work into **M**anageable **A**ctions and **D**ecisions."
- "EASY gives us the 'First Five Wins' — simple AI tasks that take under 5 minutes."
- "ACMI adds persistent memory so your AI actually knows your business over time."
- "AI Integration is the long game: workflows that run on their own, tagged and tracked."

### Script (5 min)
1. Welcome + introduce Sparkhouse Charlotte and Do Greater mission.
2. Quick intro round: name, business type, one repetitive task they'd like to eliminate. (2–3 people only — keep it tight.)
3. Present the Four Quadrants model visually on the main board.

**Core Explanation:**
> **MAD** = Structure. Break chaotic work into specific actions and decisions AI can help with.  
> **EASY** = Speed. Immediate low-effort tasks with visible results.  
> **ACMI** = Memory. Persistent profiles that keep AI aligned with your business context.  
> **AI Integration** = Autonomy. Connected workflows that operate with minimal human input.

### Interactive Exercise: Quadrant Sort (15 min)

1. Hand out sticky notes to every participant.
2. Ask each person to write **one task from their business** they performed in the last week that felt repetitive, frustrating, or easily forgettable. One task per note.
3. On the main board, draw the Four Quadrants.
4. Invite volunteers to place their sticky note in the quadrant they think it belongs to.
5. Briefly discuss 4–5 placements as a group — ask why they chose that quadrant.

**Facilitator Call-Back:**
> "Notice how most sticky notes land in MAD or EASY first. That's normal. Everything starts messy. ACMI and AI Integration are where we move things to over time, not week one."

**Transition:** "Now let's look at what 'quick win' actually looks like — so you leave today with something done."

## Lesson Block 2 (20 min): EASY in Action — Your First Five Wins

**Theme:** DO ONE THING TODAY  
**Goal:** Each participant completes one practical AI task live, using only their laptop and a free tool.

### Facilitator Talking Points

- "You don't need a developer. You don't need a budget. You just need a clear prompt and 5 minutes."
- "An EASY task has three rules: repeatable, low-stakes, and under 5 minutes."
- "We're not building a robot that runs your business. We're building a helper that takes the tedium out."

### Script (5 min)
1. Project the EASY task list on screen.
2. Walk through the first two examples live (see below).
3. Announce it's time for participants to try themselves.

**Five EASY Task Examples:**
1. **Customer reply draft** — Paste a real customer question, generate a professional response.
2. **Social caption batch** — Input a product description, output 5 captions for Instagram/LinkedIn.
3. **Meeting notes cleanup** — Paste rough notes, generate polished bullet summary.
4. **FAQ generation** — Input 3 common customer questions + answers, format into an FAQ.
5. **Invoice follow-up email** — Input client name + amount owed, generate friendly reminder.

### Interactive Exercise: Live EASY Sprint (15 min)

1. Ask participants to pick **one** of the five tasks.
2. Open a shared doc / private window to their preferred free AI tool.
3. Facilitator circulates among tables, troubleshooting prompts.
4. Ask 2–3 volunteers to share: before/after, time saved, quality check.

**Facilitator Call-Back:**
> "This is an EASY win, not a permanent system. Week two, we'll teach you to template it. Week three, we'll thread it into ACMI so it remembers your brand voice."

**Transition:** "Quick wins are great — but context is what makes AI useful long-term. That's where ACMI comes in."

## Lesson Block 3 (20 min): ACMI — Giving AI a Memory

**Theme:** STOP STARTING FROM SCRATCH  
**Goal:** Explain ACMI persistent profiles and timelines; participants draft the first section of their own business profile.

### Facilitator Talking Points

- "Most AI interactions are amnesic — every session starts fresh. Your brand voice, your customer list, your pricing: the AI forgets it all."
- "ACMI provides persistent storage: profile, signals, and timeline."
- "You don't need to build this. You seed it once, and it compounds."
- "Even a one-paragraph profile is better than starting blank every time."

### Script (5 min)
1. Present ACMI as three layers:
   - **Profile** = Your business's permanent identity (name, industry, voice, audience)
   - **Signals** = Key facts that should override generic AI assumptions
   - **Timeline** = What happened recently that AI should reference
2. Show a sample ACMI profile on screen (anonymized example from a local business).
3. Explain why timeline matters: "AI that knows your last 3 launches will draft better copy than AI that doesn't."

**Sample Profile Fields:**
- Business name + industry
- Core audience (1 sentence)
- Brand voice (3 adjectives)
- Top 3 services or products
- Any 'never say' rules
- Current active projects or campaigns

### Interactive Exercise: Profile Seed Sprint (15 min)

1. Hand out the guided worksheet with the profile fields listed.
2. Ask participants to fill in as many fields as they can in 10 minutes.
3. Tables compare notes: "What field gave you the most trouble?"
4. Facilitator collects any challenges — e.g., "My business does too many things to summarize."

**Facilitator Call-Back:**
> "If you only wrote 3 fields today, that's enough. ACMI is living — you update it as your business changes. Perfect is the enemy of started."

**Transition:** "Profile alone isn't enough. Let's connect these pieces into a plan that lasts longer than today."

## Lesson Block 4 (20 min): 30-Day Roadmap & Closing Commitment

**Theme:** LEAVE WITH A PLAN  
**Goal:** Each participant walks out with a dated, prioritized 30-day AI integration roadmap.

### Facilitator Talking Points

- "AI adoption isn't a leap — it's a ladder. Today you're on step one."
- "Your roadmap has three phases: Learn this week, Build this month, Expand this quarter."
- "Accountability matters. Pick ONE person in this room to check in with next session."

### Script (5 min)
1. Reveal the 30-day roadmap template on screen and on the shared doc.
2. Explain the structure:
   - **Week 1** = Complete one EASY task daily (build the muscle).
   - **Week 2** = Template your EASY task so it's reusable.
   - **Week 3** = Build or seed your ACMI profile.
   - **Week 4** = Connect ACMI to at least one existing workflow.
3. Show example roadmap from a sample small business owner.

### Interactive Exercise: Roadmap Builder (15 min)

1. Participants open the shared roadmap template.
2. They fill in:
   - Their name + business
   - The ONE business challenge they want to solve first
   - One EASY task for each of the next 4 weeks
   - One accountability partner name
3. Tables share roadmaps in pairs (2 minutes each).
4. Collection: ask for show-of-hands — "Who committed to a specific task this week?"

**Facilitator Call-Back:**
> "Commit to something small enough that you can't fail. 'Send one AI-drafted email this week' beats 'automate my entire business' by a mile."

### Final Wrap (last 3 minutes of block)

1. Thank participants and Sparkhouse Charlotte team.
2. Announce next workshop: Workshop 02 — MAD: Mapping Your Workflows.
3. Share contact / follow-up channel (email list, Slack, or group).
4. Invite feedback: "One word describing how you feel about AI right now."
