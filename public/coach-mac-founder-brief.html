Founder Brief — Coach Mac

The questions are the product.

Walk in empty-handed. Let Coach Mac show off SparkHouse first, then ask what excites him, what frustrates him, and what he would build if nothing were in the way.

Use the Interview Flow ↗ Formal Workshop Track ↗

The spine

I love what Do Greater is doing, and I want to help — not as a vendor, just because I believe in it. The workshop series above is the formal, billed part of this relationship. This part isn’t that.

I know systems, AI, and digital infrastructure well enough to be useful beyond a workshop room. I would rather put that time directly into SparkHouse, Amanda’s ops needs, Start Upward, Designathon, and wherever there is an actual gap.

This is the deeper relationship track: direct support for youth and community programs, not billed, not a pitch, just helpful.

Best language
“I love what Do Greater is doing, and I want to help — not as a vendor, just because I believe in it.”

Interview him. Do not pitch him.

The goal is not to prove expertise. The goal is to create the room where his real wishlist comes out.

Phase 1

Ground level — let him show off first

Start with the space, the vibe, and what is already working. He leads; trust builds before the conversation gets strategic.

  • What has been the most surprising thing since SparkHouse Uptown opened?
  • What is one thing you thought would work that did not, and one thing you did not expect that took off?
  • Of all the programs running right now, which one gets you most excited when you walk through in the morning?
Phase 2

Vision mode — where he is trying to go

Probe trajectory with genuine curiosity. This is where priorities show up without making him defend a budget or plan.

  • You have built this incredible physical hub. What is the five-year vision for Do Greater beyond the walls of SparkHouse?
  • When you think about scaling the mission, what feels like the force multiplier: more spaces, more programs, or something else?
  • If you had a magic wand and unlimited budget for the next 12 months, what is the one thing you would build that you cannot get to right now?
Phase 3

Future trends — connect dots through the mission

Frame AI, work, and SparkHouse as questions about his mission, not as a chance to show what you know.

  • You are working with the next generation. What are you seeing in how young people think about work and entrepreneurship that is different from five years ago?
  • With AI changing how work gets done, how do you see that affecting the skills you are trying to teach these kids?
  • You have a hybrid creative, tech, and maker-space model. Do you see SparkHouse as a prototype for something bigger?
  • What is your take on agentic AI: software that starts doing work instead of only acting as a tool? Has that come up for SparkHouse ops or curriculum?
Phase 4

The hook — let him pull you in

Amanda mentioned the challenge of not being able to capture people when they walk in. I have some ideas on that if you are interested. But honestly, I would rather hear what keeps you up at night operationally and see if anything I know maps to it.

Listening rules for the room

Let the wishlist surface. The magic-wand answer tells you the real priority before budget, staffing, and politics filter it down.
Make AI mission-shaped. Agentic ops lands when it is about the youth, the space, the curriculum, and Amanda's load.
Leave with his language. Capture the words he uses for the mission, the blockers, and the future. Those words become the next brief.

Two-track offer

Track 1 · Formal

Getting Started with AI for Small Business

80-minute workshop at SparkHouse, coordinated with Amanda, with Module 1 curriculum draft and venue confirmation as the immediate work item.

Track 2 · Relational

Support where there is a real gap

Show up for SparkHouse, youth programs, Amanda’s operational load, and Coach Mac’s mission context with useful observations and no hard ask.

Useful observations, softened

Google Ad Grant opportunity There is a practical grant-backed search lane for donations, volunteers, workshops, SparkHouse tours, and sponsor intent. It should be framed as free attention for the mission, not a marketing upsell.
Search Console / Google Business profile gap Ownership, indexing, and local discovery should be cleaned up so people searching for the real organization find the real doors.
Website performance and discovery issues The site needs crawler-readable definitions for DO Greater, SparkHouse, programs, locations, and partner pathways so answer engines do not confuse the organization.
SparkHouse foot traffic follow-up funnel The physical space is powerful. The missed opportunity is capturing visits, tours, events, and coffee traffic into warm follow-up for programs, sponsors, creators, and volunteers.

Quiet credibility, only when it fits

Balto and the social-story work are parallel proof of capability in youth-support and neurodivergent-child content. They should not be forced into DO Greater as a sale.

Use it only if the conversation naturally turns toward youth curriculum, accessibility, autism/ABA resources, parent-facing supports, or clinically reviewed educational content.

Relevant proof

5 elopement-safety social stories, 43 storyboard panels, ABA/BCBA clinical review passed with recommendations, WCAG review blocked on fixable contrast/text/SVG-title items, and product config prepared for digital downloads and bundle pricing.
